How Much Protein Is in an Egg? A Real Breakdown for Indian Kitchens

A
Anshul Tibrewala Founder of What to Cook. Building India-first AI nutrition tools. Based in Bangalore.

Quick answer

One large egg (50g) has about 6g protein and 70–78 kcal, split roughly 3.6g protein in the white and 2.7g in the yolk. For practical planning, use 6g protein per egg. Two eggs at breakfast gives 12g protein at under 160 kcal — one of the most efficient protein sources available in any Indian kitchen.

Eggs get recommended constantly in Indian protein conversations, but the actual numbers rarely get spelled out. Six grams sounds small next to a 100g paneer serving at 18g — until you notice an egg weighs a fifth as much and costs a fraction as much per gram of protein. Here is the full breakdown, including what actually happens to the numbers once you turn eggs into bhurji, curry, or an omelette.

The number: 6g protein per large egg

A standard large egg in India (50–55g) contains approximately 6g of protein and 70–78 kcal. Of that, the white contributes about 3.6g protein at only 17 kcal, and the yolk contributes about 2.7g protein at 55 kcal along with nearly all the fat.

For quick mental math: two eggs = 12g protein at roughly 150 kcal. Three eggs = 18g protein at roughly 220 kcal — the same protein as 100g of paneer, at 70 fewer calories.

Egg protein is also a complete protein with one of the highest biological values of any food — historically used as the reference standard against which other protein sources are measured.

Whole egg vs egg white: the real trade-off

The egg-white-only trend optimises for one thing: protein per calorie. At 3.6g protein for 17 kcal, egg white is genuinely efficient — a 4-egg-white omelette gets you 14g protein at just 68 kcal.

But the yolk isn't dead weight. It carries choline (important for liver and brain function), vitamin D, vitamin B12, lutein, and most of the flavour and satiety. A meal of only egg whites tends to leave people hungry within two hours; the fat in the yolk slows digestion and keeps you full longer.

Unless you're in a tight calorie deficit and specifically need to cut fat grams, whole eggs are the more practical everyday choice. Save whites-only for when you're already eating yolks elsewhere in the day (paneer, ghee, nuts) and want to add pure protein without more fat.

Protein by Indian preparation: what survives the cooking

The protein count in the egg itself doesn't change with cooking — it's the added oil, ghee, or other ingredients that shift the calorie math. Here's what common Indian preparations actually deliver per 2-egg serving:

• Boiled egg (2 eggs, no additions): 12g protein, 155 kcal • Egg bhurji (2 eggs, 1 tsp oil, onion-tomato-masala): 13g protein, 210 kcal • Plain omelette (2 eggs, 1 tsp oil): 12g protein, 195 kcal • Egg curry (2 eggs in a standard onion-tomato gravy, 1 serving): 13g protein, 260–300 kcal • Egg fried rice (2 eggs folded into 1 cup rice): 15g protein (including rice), 420 kcal • Anda paratha (1 egg mixed into stuffed paratha dough): 8g protein, 320 kcal

Boiled and bhurji give you the most protein per calorie. Egg curry and paratha are enjoyable but calorie-heavier because of the oil and refined carbs carrying them.

How eggs compare to paneer, chicken, and dal per rupee

Eggs are one of the cheapest protein sources available in an Indian kitchen. A rough cost-per-gram-of-protein comparison, using typical retail prices:

• Eggs: ₹6–8 per egg → about ₹1.2–1.4 per gram of protein • Chicken breast: ₹250–300/kg → about ₹0.9–1.1 per gram of protein • Paneer: ₹350–400/kg → about ₹1.9–2.2 per gram of protein • Masoor dal (cooked): ₹90–110/kg dry → about ₹1.0–1.2 per gram of protein

Chicken and dal edge out eggs slightly on pure cost-per-gram, but eggs win on convenience — no soaking, no marination, ready in under 5 minutes, and they work at any meal including breakfast, which dal and chicken rarely do well.

How many eggs should you actually eat per day?

For most healthy adults, 2–3 eggs per day is a well-supported range, and the old cholesterol-avoidance advice that capped eggs at 2–3 per week has been substantially walked back by more recent research — dietary cholesterol has a much smaller effect on blood cholesterol than saturated fat intake overall.

If you have an existing cardiovascular condition or your doctor has specifically flagged cholesterol, that guidance should override this. For everyone else, using 2–3 eggs a day as one of several protein sources — alongside dal, paneer, curd, or chicken — is a reasonable, well-tolerated approach.

A practical example: 2 eggs at breakfast (12g protein) is a strong start toward an 80g daily protein target, leaving 68g to come from lunch, snack, and dinner.

Key takeaways

  • One large egg = about 6g protein and 70–78 kcal; use this as your baseline for quick math
  • Egg white is the most calorie-efficient protein (3.6g at 17 kcal) but the yolk carries most of the nutrients and satiety
  • Boiled eggs and bhurji give the best protein-per-calorie of common Indian preparations
  • Eggs are among the cheapest protein sources in an Indian kitchen, on par with dal and chicken
  • 2–3 eggs a day is a reasonable range for most healthy adults, as one of several daily protein sources

Frequently asked questions

How much protein is in one whole egg?

A large egg (50g) contains approximately 6g protein and 70–78 kcal. The egg white alone has 3.6g protein at just 17 kcal, while the yolk has 2.7g protein at 55 kcal along with almost all the fat and micronutrients.

Is egg white or whole egg better for protein?

Egg white gives you protein at a much lower calorie cost — 3.6g protein for 17 kcal versus 6g protein for 70+ kcal in a whole egg. But the yolk carries choline, vitamin D, and most of the micronutrients, plus it makes the egg far more satiating. Unless you're specifically minimising calories, whole eggs are the better everyday choice.

Does cooking method change the protein in eggs?

The protein content of the egg itself doesn't change — boiling, frying, or scrambling doesn't add or remove protein. What changes is the calorie count, driven by added oil or ghee. A boiled egg and a fried egg both have about 6g protein, but the fried egg has 30–50 more kcal depending on how much oil is used.
